# Matrix Multiplication: The Chain Reaction

We know that a matrix is a function: it takes a vector and transforms it. But what if we want to apply *two* transformations?

Imagine you are editing a photo. First, you want to **Rotate** it by 90 degrees. Then, you want to **Shear** it.

You could do this in two steps:
1.  Apply the Rotation matrix to every pixel.
2.  Take the result and apply the Shear matrix to it.

But this is slow. Is there a way to combine these two actions into a single "Super-Matrix" that does both at once? This process of smashing two transformations into one is called **Matrix Multiplication**.

## Geometric Intuition: Tracking the Basis

To find this "Super-Matrix," we just need to follow our golden rule: **Track the Basis Vectors.**

Let's say we have two matrices:
*   $\mathbf{A}$ (The second action, e.g., Shear)
*   $\mathbf{B}$ (The first action, e.g., Rotate)

We want to find the combined matrix $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{AB}$.

To find the first column of $\mathbf{C}$, we ask:
*"If I apply $\mathbf{B}$ to $\hat{\imath}$, and then apply $\mathbf{A}$ to the result, where do I end up?"*

To find the second column of $\mathbf{C}$, we ask:
*"If I apply $\mathbf{B}$ to $\hat{\jmath}$, and then apply $\mathbf{A}$ to the result, where do I end up?"*

Matrix multiplication isn't just random arithmetic. It is **function composition**. It is calculating the final destination of the grid after a sequence of movements.

## Notation: The Order Matters

In standard arithmetic, $3 \times 5$ is the same as $5 \times 3$.
In linear algebra, **order is everything**.

$$
\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{B}
$$

This means "Apply $\mathbf{B}$ first, then apply $\mathbf{A}$."
Think of it like functions: $f(g(x))$. The function closest to the variable ($g$) happens first.

<Callout title="Non-Commutativity" type="warn">
$\mathbf{AB} \neq \mathbf{BA}$

**Try it yourself:**
1.  Rotate your phone 90 degrees. Then flip it over.
2.  Flip your phone over. Then rotate it 90 degrees.

The phone ends up in a different orientation. The order of operations changes the result.
</Callout>

## The Mechanics: Column by Column

How do we actually calculate the numbers?
Let $\mathbf{B}$ have columns $\mathbf{b}_1$ and $\mathbf{b}_2$.
The product $\mathbf{AB}$ is just $\mathbf{A}$ acting on each column of $\mathbf{B}$ separately.

$$
\mathbf{A} \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{A} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{b}_1 & \mathbf{b}_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}\mathbf{b}_1 & \mathbf{A}\mathbf{b}_2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Example:**
Let $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ (Rotate 90°)
Let $\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ (Scale by 2)

To find $\mathbf{AB}$ (Scale then Rotate):
1.  **First Column:** Apply Rotate ($\mathbf{A}$) to Scale's first col ($\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$).
    $$ \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} $$
2.  **Second Column:** Apply Rotate ($\mathbf{A}$) to Scale's second col ($\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$).
    $$ \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Result:**
$$ \mathbf{AB} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

This new matrix represents a "Rotated Scaling."

## AI/ML Relevance

<Callout title="Deep Learning is a Chain of Matrices" type="tip">
Why do we call it "Deep" Learning? Because we chain many layers together.

$$ \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}_3 ( \mathbf{W}_2 ( \mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{x} ) ) $$

If we didn't have non-linear activation functions (like ReLU) in between, all these matrices would just collapse into one single matrix $\mathbf{W}_{total} = \mathbf{W}_3\mathbf{W}_2\mathbf{W}_1$.

The power of Deep Learning comes from inserting non-linearities between these matrix multiplications, allowing the "Super-Function" to twist space in ways a single matrix never could.
</Callout>
