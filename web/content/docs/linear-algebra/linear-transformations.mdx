# Linear Transformations: Warping Space

We have vectors (data) and we have linear combinations (movement). Now, we need a way to **process** this data.

Imagine you have a photo on your phone. You want to rotate it 90 degrees. Or imagine you have a dataset of house prices, but the units are all wrong (sq ft vs meters), and you need to squash one axis to make them comparable.

You need a function. In calculus, functions take a number and give you a number ($f(x) = x^2$). In linear algebra, functions take a **vector** and give you a **vector**. These functions are called **Linear Transformations**. They don't just move one point; they move *every* point in space at the same time, keeping the grid structure intact.

## Geometric Intuition

Visually, a linear transformation is a **warp** of the 2D plane (or 3D space). Imagine the grid lines of a graph are printed on a sheet of infinite rubber fabric. A linear transformation can **stretch** the fabric, **squish** it, **rotate** it, or **shear** it (pushing the top sideways).

<Callout title="The Two Golden Rules" type="important">
To be a "Linear" transformation, the warp must follow two strict rules:
1.  **Grid lines must remain parallel and evenly spaced.** (No curving or warping like a funhouse mirror).
2.  **The Origin $(0,0)$ must stay fixed in place.** (No simply shifting the whole graph; that's called an "Affine" transformation).
</Callout>

If you follow these rules, the transformation is "Linear."

## Formal Notation: The Matrix

Here is the magic trick of linear algebra. Because the grid lines stay parallel and evenly spaced, you don't need to track where *every* point goes. You only need to track **two points**: the basis vectors.

*   $\hat{\imath} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ (The unit vector pointing Right)
*   $\hat{\jmath} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ (The unit vector pointing Up)

If you know where $\hat{\imath}$ lands and where $\hat{\jmath}$ lands, you know everything. We record these two "landing spots" in a $2 \times 2$ grid called a **Matrix**.

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
| & | \\
\text{Where } \hat{\imath} \text{ lands} & \text{Where } \hat{\jmath} \text{ lands} \\
| & |
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Symbol Dictionary
*   $\mathbf{A}$: The Transformation Matrix (the function).
*   $\mathbf{x}$: The input vector.
*   $\mathbf{Ax}$: The output vector (transformed).
*   $\hat{\imath}, \hat{\jmath}$: The basis vectors of our input space.

## Simple Worked Example

Let's perform a **Shear**. We want to keep $\hat{\imath}$ exactly where it is, but push $\hat{\jmath}$ to the right.

1.  **Where does $\hat{\imath}$ go?** It stays at $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
2.  **Where does $\hat{\jmath}$ go?** It moves from $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ to $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ (Right 1, Up 1).

We build our matrix $\mathbf{A}$ by simply plugging these columns in:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, let's see where this matrix takes a random point, say $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

$$
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
= 2 \cdot (\text{New } \hat{\imath}) + 1 \cdot (\text{New } \hat{\jmath})
$$

$$
= 2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

The point $(2, 1)$ has been sheared over to $(3, 1)$.

## AI/ML Relevance

<Callout title="Neural Networks are just Chain-Linked Matrices" type="tip">
When you look at a Deep Neural Network diagram, you see layers of neurons connected by lines.

Mathematically, each layer is just a **Linear Transformation** (a matrix multiplication) followed by a non-linear squish (activation function).

$$ \mathbf{y} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}) $$

*   $\mathbf{x}$ is your data.
*   $\mathbf{W}$ is the **Weight Matrix**.

Training a neural network is just searching for the right Matrix $\mathbf{W}$ that twists and stretches your data so that "Cats" end up on one side of the graph and "Dogs" end up on the other.
</Callout>
